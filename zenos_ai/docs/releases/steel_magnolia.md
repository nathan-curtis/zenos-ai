# Release Notes — 2026.9.0 'Steel Magnolia'

**Status:** RC1 — ETA Saturday, 2026-09-05
**Branch:** `feat/2026.9.0`
**Base:** 2026.8.0 'Chef'

## Summary

Hospitality starts with knowing what's actually going on. Who's in the room, whether they're staying the night or just passing through, what they're allergic to, whether the door that just opened is a person walking in or a raccoon setting off a motion sensor. You can't host well on guesses.

Steel Magnolia is the release where the house learns to tell the difference — and then, having figured it out, gets out of the way. A good host doesn't narrate the effort. The room is already warm, the guest's allergy is already flagged before anyone asks, the task is already sitting in the queue before you'd have remembered to write it yourself. Everything that makes that possible happens under the waterline. You don't get to see the duck paddling.

But a good host also knows how to say no. Good fences make good neighbors — and good fences don't have to be mean about it. A locked door stays locked without anyone standing guard over it. A guest gets exactly the access a stay calls for, nothing assumed, nothing left open by accident. None of it reads as a checkpoint, because none of it should. The house holds its boundaries the same quiet way it holds its warmth — steadily, automatically, without making a production of it.

Room Manager v3 is the clearest expression of the first half of that idea: a room that genuinely knows what state it's in, not by watching a clock, but by reading what's actually happening in the space right now. Twenty CRM's structured stay/appointment objects, and the new arrival-prep and checkout-nudge scans built on top of them, are the second half — a guest's stay is tracked start to finish, prefs and all, and the house acts on that timeline instead of just logging it.

The rest of this release is the plumbing that makes both halves trustworthy: a shared fan-out collector so twenty-odd hand-rolled scan loops become one audited pattern, a real fix for a fail-open permission stub before anything ever called it, a stale-triage signal that used to evaporate into nothing now landing as a real task. ZQ-1 tells you when a query silently found nothing because you asked the wrong question instead of because the data doesn't exist. Recorder stats stopped lying about energy sensors. None of it is glamorous. All of it is why the glamorous parts can be trusted.

## Room Manager v3 — The Living Room-State Engine

Room Manager reads a room the way a person would — not "has X minutes elapsed," but "what's actually happening in here right now." See [Room Manager v3 & REFLEX](../architecture/22_Room_Manager_v3_REFLEX.md) for the design doc and [the operator's manual](../getting_started/room_manager_operators_manual.md) for the plain-language version.

- **`room_control_manager`** — a dedicated control surface, `select.<room>_control_manager`, one cabinet-backed dict drawer keyed by room instead of a per-room helper entity. The select fires an event on `select_option`; a single dispatch listener is the sole writer. Named by ownership, not version — so a future revision never has to force a migration off it.

- **Wasp-in-a-box, for real.** A `wasp_door` opening floors the room to `occupied` immediately and clears any latched hold. Motion firing while *every* `wasp_door` for the room is closed sets `wasp_flag_active`, and the cascade reports `hold` instead of confidently guessing `occupied` — because there's a real question mark there: something moved, but nothing came in or out. The flag is a self-referencing template attribute, not a new per-room helper, and it clears on exactly two events, never a timeout: a fresh door open, or a manual `room_control_manager` override. It's the box the cat's either in or isn't — the room just refuses to pretend it knows which, until a door tells it.
  - `mode=label_discover`'s `group_warnings` catches a lock+door pair tagged for the same opening and flags it — tag the door (`wasp_door`), never the lock.

- **`entertaining_hold` / `guest_hold`** — two `hold` sources, opt-in by label, scoped to exactly the rooms that want them. Tag the household's existing `input_boolean.zen_entertaining` (or `zen_guest_mode`) with a purpose label plus the target room's own label — nothing new to create. Both outrank `occupied`/`vacant`, both outranked by `engaged`/`asleep`/manual override/emergency.

- **Asleep window — the room knows what time it thinks it is.** A direct `asleep`/`bed_occupancy` signal only resolves to `asleep` inside the household's real scheduler-anchor clock (`zen_night_start`/`zen_am_start`) — a hamper on a bed at 2pm doesn't put a room to sleep. Gated identically at every point that independently derives asleep truthiness, so nothing silently disagrees with itself. Asleep-class room timers default to a real 8 hours.

- **`autosleep_schedule`** — for rooms where "night" isn't at night. Shift workers, or anyone whose sleep schedule doesn't track the house's clock. Tag any truthy-resolving entity (a toggle, a calendar, an HA `schedule.*` helper) with `autosleep_schedule` plus the room's own label, and it becomes **authoritative** for that room — fully replacing the night→wake window check, not widening it. `asleep_window_disable` still outranks it if a room somehow carries both labels.

- **`asleep_hold`** — a third, independent path into Asleep. Structurally identical to `entertaining_hold`/`guest_hold`, but feeds `asleep` directly instead of `hold`. Tag a truthy-resolving entity with `asleep_hold` plus the room's label and the room reads `asleep` outright — no bed sensor, no trigger signal, no window check needed. Clears the instant the tagged entity goes false, or a manual override.

**Cascade order:**
```
emergency > manual override (room_control_manager) > asleep > engaged >
child-engaged > hold (wasp / entertaining / guest) > occupied (or fridge-door hold) > vacant
```

**Setup note:** six labels (`entertaining_hold`, `guest_hold`, `autosleep_disable`, `asleep_window_disable`, `autosleep_schedule`, `asleep_hold`) live in HA's label registry, not in git — the feature is a safe no-op until you create them: `zen_dojotools_labels mode=create label_list=["entertaining_hold","guest_hold","autosleep_disable","asleep_window_disable","autosleep_schedule","asleep_hold"] confirm=true`.

- **`domain_routing` gains a `crm` entry.** Twenty CRM's vendor/contact tooling was fully built and live but had no entry in Room Manager's own domain-routing table, making it undiscoverable through the path most callers actually use to find the right tool for a room.
- **Zone-mode disambiguation.** `zone_set`/`zone_remove`/`zones` renamed to `room_zone_set`/`room_zone_remove`/`room_zones` — the name collided with `zen_dojotools_zones`, the real HA `zone.*` geofencing tool, though the two share no other overlap (RM's zones are sub-room spatial pockets like "reading nook"). Zero external callers found, zero-blast-radius rename.

## Room Manager v3 — Wasp Gate, Coverage Tooling, and REFLEX Correctness

- **Wasp/hold rewritten again, this time to pure physical-space semantics.** No timer, no self-latch — true iff a `wasp_door` exists, the room opted into a new `wasp_enabled` gate, no `wasp_door` is currently open, and occupied/motion is live. The gate exists because rooms with an open archway instead of a real door (no way to tell "someone's inside with it shut" from "there is no door") would otherwise misfire constantly.
- **~14 new purpose-trigger platform entries** (HA 2024.9+) scoped by label instead of static entity enumeration — an additional live-signal source layered on top of the existing explicit `trigger_entities` list, not a replacement for it.
- **New diagnostic modes**: `trigger_audit` (which classes are purpose-trigger-covered vs. still need static wiring), `coverage_map` (unified diagnostic combining label-discovery gaps, trigger coverage, dormant-feature detection, and wasp-gate status — advisory only, never auto-applies), `wasp_enable` (read/write the room-level gate).
- **`room_occupancy` cabinet mirror** — every real room-state transition now also writes that room's full attribute set to the household cabinet, a read-surface companion to the native per-room sensors (which stay authoritative). Caught and fixed a real concurrency bug in the same pass: two rooms transitioning simultaneously raced on a shared dict drawer and clobbered each other; fixed with FileCabinet's nested-key support so each room gets an independent key.
- **REFLEX dry-run was gated backwards.** REFLEX on used to always fire for real even with dry-run also on — the one case dry-run is most useful for. REFLEX off with dry-run on used to still produce dry-run logs for a system that would never fire anything. Fixed across all 3 fire sites (the main scene resolver plus both nightlight paths, the latter two of which had no dry-run check at all): REFLEX off means nothing happens, period; REFLEX on + dry-run on simulates only, zero real side effects; REFLEX on + dry-run off fires for real.
- **Retired `checking` state fully purged** from every remaining live reference (label registries, diagnostic state lists, help text) — it had already been dropped from the core cascade but was still lingering as a "valid" entry in half a dozen other places.
- **Tool self-description stabilization**: `mode=help` was missing ~10 real modes entirely; REFLEX's explanation lived in one unstructured prose blob while every other mode got a clean one-liner; `reflex_assignments` converted from pipe-delimited strings to structured JSON, matching every other multi-field write in the tool. A follow-up UAT pass caught a third, independent bug — `tool_manifest` carries its own separate mode-description dict from `help`'s, already drifted, missing `reflex_dry_run` entirely.

## Room Manager v3 — Timer Correctness, Trouble Class, and Cascade Fixes

A late batch of live-hunted fixes, all landing in 9.0 rather than being held for 10.0 — Room Manager is core enough to this release's premise that anything genuinely broken in it ships now, beta or not.

- **`room_timer_class` internal helper eliminated.** A per-room `input_select` existed purely to hold a 3-value internal tag (`occupied`/`engaged`/`asleep`) alongside each room's real `timer.<room>_room_timer` — pure bookkeeping, never meant for a human to see or touch, exactly the class of unnecessary helper entity this system is supposed to avoid. Migrated onto `room_topology[area].room_timer_class`, a plain field on the same cabinet object `room_control` already lives on. The real `timer.*` entity is untouched; only the class tag moved. ~19 per-room package files had their now-orphaned `input_select:` declaration removed.
- **Hold-release now restarts the suspended timer.** Every hold flavor (wasp, `entertaining_hold`, `guest_hold`, manual Hold) cancels a room's timer the instant it latches — but until now, nothing ever restarted one when the hold released, so an occupied room fell straight through to Vacant even if someone was still there, just not actively re-triggering a sensor at that exact instant. Fixed by remembering the suspended timer class (free, since nothing ever cleared it) and re-arming it on the room's own `room_state_changed` event when a hold releases. `asleep` is deliberately excluded — it releases plainly, by design.
- **Held-room guard extended to two more timer-arming sites**, plus the engaged-timer-expiry handoff for consistency — a wasp-door edge event or the periodic house-wide timer reconcile pass could each independently re-arm Occupied underneath an already-held room; both now check the same `_room_currently_held` guard the motion-evidence branch already had.
- **Generic `hold` label now resolves to a real state.** Previously it only floored a room at `occupied` — a level check with no memory. Moved into the same cascade branch as the other hold sources, ahead of `occupied`. Zero blast radius for existing installs (nothing was tagged with the bare label before this).
- **New generic `trouble` label + `trouble_active` attribute** — an ATTRIBUTE overlay, never a cascade state, so a hot breaker in one room can't flip a sleeping bedroom's state. Fully generic tag socket, plus one native source with zero tagging required: for any room whose circuits are exposed via a current + breaker-rating provider (SPAN Panel qualifies; Emporia Vue does not — power/energy only, no amps), the blueprint independently computes per-circuit draw vs. rated capacity and sets `trouble_active` if any circuit sustains over 80% (the NFPA/NEC continuous-load threshold) — computed live inside the room sensor itself, no recorder/history dependency.
- **New exposed attributes**: `motion_live_active` (raw level-check, distinct from `occupied_active` which can stay true on a decaying timer long after motion cleared), `smoke_active`, `leak_active`, `trouble_active`, `occupancy_evidence_source` (physical/automation/person — captured from real state-change context on the five occupancy-driving triggers, observability only, feeds nothing in the cascade), plus the actual offending entity_id (not just a boolean) for garage/window-covering/exterior-lock conditions.
- **Cross-room label sharing**, now a documented technique: one sensor's entity can carry multiple rooms' own labels when it's genuinely the shared boundary for several logical rooms — a single entry door as the enclosure boundary for a nested suite, or one open-floor-plan smoke detector covering several adjacent rooms.
- **Adjustable blind period after a wasp release.** A quick door open/close cycle right after a release could otherwise re-latch wasp immediately. The exposed flag now stays false for a configurable window (`wasp_blind_seconds`, default 5s, per-room override via labeled `input_number`) after release, even though the underlying physical condition can go true again immediately.
- **Paused and Emergency now cascade unconditionally through nested rooms** — the existing occupied/engaged child-cascade can be opted out per room (`child_link_disable`), but a safety all-stop or life-safety event in a child room should never be silently suppressible by that toggle. Propagates naturally up a multi-level chain with no explicit walk, since each level's own resolved state already reflects its children's cascade.
- **A real Jinja truthy bug, caught live within seconds of deploying**: an early version of the wasp self-latch expression used an `{% if %}/{% elif %}/{% else %}` block instead of a single `{{ }}` ternary — HA's Jinja only native-type-casts a bare single-expression template, so the block form rendered the literal string `"false"`, which is truthy. Every room with `child_rooms` or wasp capability got stuck permanently in `Hold`. Reverted to the ternary form the file already uses everywhere else.
- **Cleaning-dispatch bridge fixed for real.** The resync pass that's supposed to reflect a vacuum's presence into `room_control: Cleaning` was writing to a legacy `room_control_manager` select entity nothing in a modern install creates — dead code since the real write path moved to the cabinet-backed `room_control_set` mechanism weeks earlier. Fixed to use the real path; confirmed live. New behavior: manually setting a room to `Cleaning` by hand is no longer a passive label — if no vacuum is actually there, the same pass now dispatches one (when it's genuinely free) instead of just clearing the stale flag. Opt-out via an `autovac_manual_dispatch_disable`-tagged `input_boolean`.

## AutoVac — Roborock Bug Fixes and a Severe Election-Gate Bug

A mid-cycle vacuum hardware swap (`xiaomi_miio` → core `roborock`) surfaced a surprising amount of latent breakage in AutoVac itself — real bugs, not new integration work, all fixed and live-verified rather than just config-check-clean.

- **A severe bug, live since the DND election gate shipped: it never actually passed, for anyone.** Both the automatic election loop and `mode=clean`'s explicit check resolved a room's v3 state via `area_entities()`, which requires a literal HA Area assignment — but v3 room-state sensors are template entities organized entirely by label, with no Area. The resolved state always fell back to `'unknown'`, which never equals `'vacant'`, so the gate silently blocked every automatic election and every explicit clean call from ever reaching a genuinely vacant room, indefinitely. Found via a live Friday/Assist interaction that correctly diagnosed the contradiction. Fixed by switching to `label_entities()`, the convention every other room resolution in this codebase already uses.
- **Battery, wear, and consumables tracking broke silently on the new integration and are now fixed.** `roborock` splits battery into its own sensor instead of a `vacuum` platform attribute HA has since deprecated — added a labeled-sensor-first fallback chain that never silently reads missing data as 0%. Wear sensors changed both name and unit (percent remaining → raw hours remaining) — fixed across all 7 `roborock_*` presets with a new `wear_kind` field, existing `xiaomi_miio` presets untouched. Also fixed a separate, pre-existing bug found along the way: `mode=consumables action=provision` computed the full catalog but never actually wrote it to the cabinet.
- **Room targeting was silently wrong the whole time this branch existed** — `vacuum.clean_area`'s real field is `cleaning_area_id`, not `areas` (confirmed against HA core's own service schema), never exercised until `roborock` went live and exposed it.
- **Manual-run room tracking was a real gap, now closed**: a run started outside AutoVac (the Roborock app, the vacuum tile) left `analyze` with nothing to stamp `last_cleaned` with. New tracker follows the vacuum's real per-room sensor, bridged via a new minimal blueprint (`zen_cleaning_dispatch.yaml`) since no HA purpose-trigger exists for an arbitrary enum sensor. Also fixed a bug where a Roborock mop-wash pit-stop mid-run (a genuine dock event, same as a real finish) used to silently discard rooms already tracked before the pit-stop.
- **False "untracked run" alarm eliminated.** This dock genuinely docks twice at the end of a real run — once to wash the mop, then again to charge. A second dock event within 5 minutes of a run's own finish timestamp is now recognized as the expected echo instead of misreporting a real, already-tracked run as lost.
- **Camera/map analysis follows the vacuum's own coverage-map domain** (`image.*` under `roborock`, not `camera.*` — neither of HA's camera-only shortcuts work on that domain), with a conservative, iterated analysis prompt that stays under-report-over-guess after an earlier pass induced hallucinated room names.
- **Error handling now routes through AlertManager** instead of paging directly — respects quiet-hours policy by default (a stuck vacuum no longer pushes at 2am the same as noon) and dedupes by alert key.
- **New `dry_run` support on `mode=clean`**, matching what `run_elected` already had — runs every real validation and reports what would happen without ever calling the vacuum service.

## Cross-Cutting Fixes — Essence Bootstrap, Twenty CRM, and Taskmaster

- **A real, already-live essence-corruption bug, found and fixed.** Multiple bootstrap paths (Flynn's stepgate sentinel, the OOBE protocol, the bootstrap content seed-check) read a persona's name via the legacy `identity.name` shape only — a real `three_layer` essence (core/jacket/companion) keeps the name at `jacket.name` instead. Any of these paths reading a healthy signed three-layer essence saw an empty name, concluded it was unseeded, and silently stomped it with defaults — exactly what happened to Friday's own essence on the reference install (lost signature, relationship-arc data, directives), restored from the automatic `_prev` snapshot. A second, older bug in the same area was fixed alongside it: `write` mode never stamped the field `sign` mode hard-requires, so signing had likely never actually succeeded on that install even before this incident.
- **Twenty CRM's `stays_active_list` had no date-range check at all** — a reserved stay whose checkout had already passed, but was never explicitly released, counted as "active" indefinitely. A stale reservation from 18 days prior was confirmed still reporting as current. Fixed to also require checkout be blank or in the future.
- **A second, independent bug one layer up**: Taskmaster's household briefing was still reporting a stale guest as present even after the above fix, because it read guest presence from an entirely separate calendar-based path that had been mistakenly pointed at a generic catch-all calendar. Switched to the corrected, date-scoped CRM lookup.
- **A real self-sustaining alert loop, running since 2026-08-22, diagnosed and broken.** Two ambient summarizer components were narrating alarm about each other's own auto-filed health tickets in a closed loop — a classic self-referential-accumulator shape. Fixed by excluding the pipeline's own self-generated ticket stamp from Taskmaster's ingestion, breaking the loop at its only real chokepoint.
- **New `catch_up` mode** for recurring daily items (medications, chores) — no server-side "skip to today" action exists in the underlying task platform's API, so this rolls `due_date` forward directly for anything genuinely overdue, without touching completion state or recurrence.
- **Conductor items now count toward briefing's overdue/due-today/stale totals** — previously excluded from scoring (correctly, they're unscored by design) but also invisible to the totals, so a conductor item could sit overdue for weeks with zero visibility anywhere in the briefing.

## Hospitality Lifecycle — Arrival, Stay & Departure

A guest stay is a real, structured object from the moment you tell the house about it to the moment the room's turned over — not a calendar entry, not a note somebody has to remember to act on.

- **Arrival prep, before anyone's at the door.** Once a stay is on the books, the house checks in on it as the arrival window approaches — allergen/music/spa prefs pulled from the guest's own record, a real task raised for whoever's handling turnover, no push-notification spam for something that isn't urgent yet. Fires once per stay; asking twice doesn't double the work.
- **Checkout, with a heads-up instead of a surprise.** The same shape, mirrored for departure — a nudge lands as checkout approaches, tagged to the room, worded in plain local time ("Wed Aug 19, 6:53 PM," not a UTC timestamp nobody asked for). It doesn't replace the actual turnover dispatch that fires on checkout itself — it just means nobody's caught flat-footed by a clock they weren't watching.
- **One source of truth for who's actually here.** Kitchen's allergen flagging, guest-aware meal planning, and the arrival/checkout scans above all read from the same shared occupant-prefs lookup — a guest's stated allergens show up everywhere that matters, once, instead of five systems each guessing separately.
- **Ask the house directly.** A stay's status — who's arriving, who's leaving, how soon — is a live, queryable fact, not something you have to wait on a notification for. If you want to know whether anyone's checking out today, ask; the answer doesn't require a push to already have fired.
- Guest identity and permission delegation — an owner granting a guest their own scoped access rather than relying on one household-wide guest flag — is explicitly out of scope this release. Held for the next one.

## Good Fences — Boundaries Without the Attitude

Hospitality and security aren't in tension. A house that holds its boundaries quietly is easier to relax in, not harder — nobody has to think about the fence because the fence is just there, doing its job.

- **Every gated action actually checks now.** A handful of write paths existed with the intent to be gated but weren't wired all the way through — declared, described, never enforced. Closed this release: staging a scene now genuinely requires the cert it was always supposed to require (`scene_handling`), regardless of which individual devices happen to be in the room. The fence isn't decorative.
- **New permission surfaces default closed, not open.** Where a feature's eligibility check hadn't been built out yet, it now fails safely by default instead of quietly waving everything through — Postman's `agent_auth` stub is the case in point, flipped fail-closed before anything ever called it. Nothing gets *more* restrictive for existing use — it just means a half-finished gate can't accidentally become a hole.
- **A stale signal used to just vanish. Now it lands somewhere.** Low-priority "this needs a human's judgment" flags that weren't urgent enough for a push and didn't have an automatable fix now surface as a real Taskmaster task instead of disappearing into a dispatcher stub that was never finished. Quiet doesn't mean ignored.

Manifest gains audit/inference fields and missing-label detection, closing the gap between what a household's tools claim to expose and what's actually wired up.

Five of Manifest's own introspection modes — the ones that fan out across every tool in the house and aggregate what comes back — now share one collector instead of each hand-rolling its own scan loop. Same behavior, verified output-for-output against the pre-change baseline; the win is entirely underneath: one audited pattern instead of five near-identical ones drifting slowly apart.

- **`dependencies=[]`** joins `inference{}` as a field every tool always reports on `tool_manifest()`, defaulting to empty. A new `mode=toolmap` walks it into a declared (not observed) dependency graph — most of the roster still reports self-contained simply because it hasn't declared anything yet; the shape is proven on a five-tool set from this release's own work.
- **A compliance blind spot found and closed.** The admintools tier had been silently excluded from every audit-mode scan target since 2026-07-29 — a crash workaround that should have fixed the underlying crash instead of routing around it. Every audit mode now scans the full tool roster, admintools included, for the first time.
- **`mount_status` was reporting every cabinet as unmounted, always** — a wrong-depth cabinet read plus a loop bug that kept re-inspecting the same entity. Both fixed.

## Query, Recorder & Prompt Correctness

- **ZQ-1 filter validation** — `filter_json` keys that don't exist (a stray `domains` instead of `domain`) used to vanish silently, producing a confidently-empty result indistinguishable from a real zero-hit query. Both `dry_run` and live responses now carry a `warning` field naming the bad key.
- **State-class-aware history stats** — requesting `mean`/`min`/`max` against a `total`/`total_increasing` energy sensor (SPAN, Emporia Vue) used to silently return empty buckets; the recorder call now branches on `state_class`. New `period_total_24h` gives a direct answer for "how much did this use today," cross-checked against recorder's own `sum` statistic and flagged when the two disagree by more than 2x.
- **`sensor.home_overview` gains a real `rooms` attribute.** Friday's live prompt template had expected this attribute since the mechanism was built; it never existed, so the room-state section of every prompt had silently rendered empty. Fixed lean, per-room, one flat `{room: state}` dict.

## Identity Gates — Locks, Covers, Security, Room Manager, ZenLux, Climate, Spa, and a Real Self-Escalation Hole Closed

A new pattern lands this release: certain actuations are risky enough that "the agent is allowed to call this tool" isn't sufficient on its own. Four tools — `zen_dojotools_locks` (new), `zen_dojotools_covers`, `zen_dojotools_security_manager`, and `zen_dojotools_infra`'s container-control codex — now gate their riskiest actions behind a certification the household grants explicitly, with the highest-risk actions additionally requiring a fresh live yes/no every single time, no standing exception.

- **`zen_dojotools_locks` — new.** Room-targetable lock/unlock for the `lock.*` domain, the gap `zen_dojotools_covers` already had closed for `cover.*`. Full Keymaster code-slot detail on inspect — PINs are never surfaced, only whether a slot is set. Real parent/child Keymaster sync topology, found after three wrong turns (Z-Wave's bulk config-parameter service doesn't work for this; Keymaster's own entities aren't area-assigned or registered under a discoverable integration domain the obvious way).
- **A self-escalation hole, found and closed.** The tool that *grants* a certification had zero gating of its own — any caller could certify itself for anything, including the very capability an identity gate was built the same session to protect. Closed with a live cert catalog (calculated fresh from every tool's own self-declaration, not a hand-maintained file that can silently drift out of sync) plus a mandatory fresh live household-admin acknowledgment on every grant or revoke.
- **`zen_dojotools_covers` gets the same treatment.** New asymmetric-risk cert: closing any cover is cert-only, opening a barrier-class cover (garage door, exterior door, gate) requires the cert *and* a fresh live ack every call, opening an ordinary window covering stays cert-only. A real pre-existing bug surfaced building the gate logic: `position=0` (a legitimate "close" call) was being silently coerced to "no position given" by a Jinja idiom that treats any falsy value as unset, not just a genuinely missing one.
- **`zen_dojotools_security_manager` gets the same treatment.** Arming and alert-policy changes require the cert; disarming — the highest-risk direction — additionally requires the fresh live ack every time, no standing shortcut.
- **A scoped admin override**, added deliberately after the zero-trust default proved right in principle but heavy-handed in practice: a household admin can exempt specific targets (a specific door, a specific container) from the every-call live-ack requirement, without weakening the cert requirement itself or reopening the self-escalation gap. Granting the exemption still requires the same live ack as any other certification change.
- **A shared "get a live yes/no" chokepoint**, extracted after the same fire-and-poll pattern turned up hand-rolled independently in four different places. The extraction itself surfaced two more real bugs: the default notification path had never actually been capable of capturing a response since it was first written, and the dispatch-success check was testing for a response key that the underlying dispatch call never actually returns — both fixed at the single shared point rather than in each of the four call sites separately.
- **A canonical response-shape pilot** (`envelope()`) separating "did the call itself succeed" from "what did the tool actually find" — two real tools converted as the first adopters. Building it surfaced its own real bug: one malformed entry in an unrelated internal queue could have silently broken every tool response using the new shape, not just the one call that touched the bad entry; fixed before it shipped anywhere.
- **Room Manager v3 gains BLE-informed occupancy** (informational only this phase — doesn't yet feed any state decision) and a purpose-neutral vibration signal class with an anomaly-gated "the load is done" detector for laundry-style use cases, filtering out a brief bump from being mistaken for a finished cycle.
- **A real, already-shipped guidance gap corrected**: agent-assisted new-room deployment previously suggested an agent with file-write access could write configuration directly into a live package file as an exception case. That's wrong regardless of what access a given session happens to have — corrected to route through a proper copy-paste deployment template instead.
- **Scope resolution centralized to one place.** Every cert-gated tool was independently re-implementing "is this target covered by an admin's scope exemption" — five copies of the same logic that wouldn't have survived the deny-list ACL format landing later without five separate rewrites. Replaced with a shared macro; every gated tool now asks identity instead of computing it locally. Surfaced an unrelated bug along the way: an invalid input-selector config mode had taken an entire core tool offline with no error surfaced anywhere, found only by bisecting against a known-good copy.
- **Room Manager gains its own identity gate.** Moving a room out of a human-set Paused state now requires a certification and, like the highest-risk actions elsewhere, a fresh live acknowledgment every time unless the specific room is covered by a granted scope exemption — the "only a human can un-pause it" rule this release already documented is now actually enforced, not just intended.
- **ZenLux gets real switch control and a full rewrite.** `light.*`-only control was a real, if unnoticed, gap — every write action called light-domain services exclusively even where a switch was the logical target. Now supports `switch.*` directly (domain-agnostic toggle, explicit switch set, full room/role resolution), gained its own identity gate, absorbed the scene-staging workaround for a real HA persistence limitation (relocated in from Room Manager, on the principle that a write belongs with the tool that owns the domain it's acting on), and converted to the same single-entry/single-exit response shape as the rest of this release's gated tools. Two generic risks surfaced doing that conversion by hand, worth knowing for anyone doing the same kind of rewrite: a validation guard's early result can be silently overwritten if the code that follows it isn't explicitly told to skip when a result already exists, and a config-check pass does not catch a control block sitting at the wrong YAML indentation level — syntactically valid, semantically dead, only caught by comparing against a known-good conversion or by actually testing it live.
- **A real deny primitive, not just allow-scoping.** `cert_scope` entries can now explicitly deny a specific target, hard-blocked ahead of the certification check with no live ack ever offered — distinct from an unscoped target, which still asks normally. A second grant for an already-held certification now merges into the existing scope instead of silently replacing it (a real data-loss risk in the earlier version: adding one new exemption used to wipe every prior one unless the caller re-sent all of them), with a dedicated per-entry removal path that doesn't require re-granting the whole certification.
- **The climate tool gets real auth and inherits `fan.*`.** Zero gating before this release — any caller could actuate a thermostat freely. New cert-only gate, no live-ack tier (nothing here is exterior-security-grade). Also closes a real, previously-unnoticed gap: there was no general-purpose fan control primitive anywhere in the codebase, only a hot-tub tool's hardcoded internal fan calls. The climate tool now owns `fan.*` directly — speed, preset, oscillation, direction — with the same fail-closed-on-unsupported-feature discipline it already applied to thermostats.
- **A full audit for identity-gate end-runs found two real gaps and closed both.** ZenZork's text-adventure commands (`open`/`close`/`use`/`push`/`pull`) were issuing raw lock/cover service calls directly against real household hardware, completely bypassing the certification and live-ack requirements those domains now enforce everywhere else — a game command could open a real garage door with zero identity check. Rerouted through the same gated tools, and — since a game session has no legitimate path to a real live acknowledgment — every such action now runs in preview mode by default, narrating from the real authorization check without ever performing the real actuation. The spa/hot-tub tool had the identical shape of gap on jets, lights, water temperature, and its cover, closed the same way as everywhere else in this release.
- **Room Manager's own write surface joins the gated tools**, two certifications covering roughly 35 modes: structural topology edits, and behavioral/state-machine writes. Both read-open, cert-only, deny-hard-blockable. The one asymmetry preserved deliberately: setting a room to Paused (increasing caution) never needs permission; only taking it back out of Paused does.
- **`scene_handling` — declared but never actually enforced, now gated for real.** `mode=scene_stage` snapshots and restores a whole room's light/cover/climate/media/switch/fan/humidifier state, but had never been added to the write-mode list that triggers the room-lock guard or any cert check — the certification existed on paper only. Gated at the action level, same shape as the other room-wide write certs: a caller needs `scene_handling` regardless of which domains' own certs it separately holds.
- **CertAdmin — a new admin-only cert authority.** `cert_grant`/`cert_revoke`/`cert_list` moved out of the agent-facing persona editor into a new admintools-only tool, never reachable by an agent even via MCP. Enables a genuinely new capability on top: an admin can waive the live-ack requirement for a specific cert (e.g. "don't ask every time I open the garage"), gated behind three independent layers — live-ack, `confirm_action`, and an exact-phrase acknowledgment — without softening live-ack for anyone else. The persona editor's own schema has nowhere to declare the waiver fields, so there's structurally nothing for an agent to pass even if it tried.
- **A script-entity naming trap, caught live.** An approved cert grant hard-failed with `ServiceNotFound` on a `script:`-platform tool whose `entity_id` had drifted from its `unique_id` after an old alias cleanup — a class of drift `automation:` entities structurally can't have, and one no reliable Jinja-side guard exists for in this HA version. Fixed by the household admin renaming the entity by hand; mitigated going forward with a new static `mode=troubleshooting` on the help tool (fast diagnosis, not prevention) plus defense-in-depth fault isolation at the persona-editor→certadmin boundary.
